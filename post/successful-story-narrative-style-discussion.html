Successful Story Narrative Style Discussion

I haven't read this book "After Topping the Imperial Examination" yet. I have only read the preface, which was written by her mentor. After reading it, I feel deeply touched: the success of ordinary people is superior to the success of the elite.

I don't think my perspective is correct, but I find it interesting and I wanted to share it with you. I also wanted to hear your thoughts on this.

Why did this idea come to my mind first? Maybe I was a bit jealous, after all, I also wanted to research this topic from a sociological perspective if I were a graduate student at Fudan University.

Of course, jealousy is not the main point. The main point is that when I read the praises her mentor had for her in the preface, it reminded me of a teacher in my freshman year who admired me and had nothing but positive things to say about me.

The narrative style in the preface reminded me of success stories of accomplished individuals. I believe they all have something in common: they encounter setbacks, gain insights/wisdom or good luck, overcome obstacles, and achieve success.

To be honest, I have always been fond of this narrative style since junior high school. I feel like I am the protagonist in those stories. I even feel like I am the one who is outstanding, not someone like Steve Jobs. However, this perception has a huge deviation. Success stories are shared after becoming successful, not during or before success, or even after failure.

Of course, success or failure depends on various factors, sometimes it's ability, sometimes it's opportunity. I know and accept this. But if success is attributed solely to one aspect, such as personal ability or willpower, it is unreasonable and a form of misleading. It's like shooting a target and then drawing a circle around it, which lacks persuasiveness.

However, I have also come to understand that the intention behind telling these stories is different. Maybe they are meant for publicity or pursuing other hidden motives; anyway, it is not my intention to truly and completely present the process of achieving success. One is the process of a successful event, the other is the process of an event becoming successful.

Furthermore, when talking about entrepreneurship, not only should we look at success stories, but also failures. I don't think it's reasonable to say that you can only understand why something succeeds by looking at failures, but by looking at failures, you can understand why something failed.

In comparison to this dominant perspective on success, paying more attention to failures can better reflect the complete picture of events. Or directly studying history, which includes both successes and failures, is the best way to understand the whole picture.

If we set aside the reasons behind storytelling and solely discuss success, I believe the success of ordinary people is superior to the success of elites. The former refers to those whose abilities and conditions are not mature, whereas the latter is the opposite. The former has to consider a lot more things, such as risks and limitations, and won't win as easily as depicted in stories unless the person is as strategic as Zhuge Liang. Just like Liu Bei and Cao Cao, the difference in their initial strengths made it difficult for Liu Bei to win.

Additionally, even if we don't compare two different individuals, and instead compare an individual with themselves, I believe accomplishing the same task under disadvantaged circumstances is more impressive than accomplishing it under advantageous circumstances. For example, if the conditions for studying are better at school than at home, reading and exercising will be easier. Although it's the same activity, jumping rope, the effort required is significantly different.

Moreover, based on practical experience, I also prefer the success of ordinary people, who succeed despite limited resources and conditions. I'm not saying that recklessness is the way to go, but rather adding 5-10% additional challenges within existing conditions, which is more in line with practicality.

Firstly, it ensures that the task is done well; secondly, it enhances one's abilities; thirdly, it simulates high-difficulty and complex situations, preparing for future challenges; fourthly, it allows for exploration of new methods and a broader perspective. After accomplishing the task, there is also a greater sense of achievement.

I saw that you are reading this book on WeChat Reading, and coincidentally, I have also read some parts of it before. I originally intended to finish reading the book and then discuss it with you, but I couldn't resist and started talking to you about the content of the preface.
